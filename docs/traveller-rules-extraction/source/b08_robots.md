# B08: Robots — Rules Extraction

## Source
Classic Traveller Book 8: Robots (1986), GDW
Design: Joe D. Fugate, Sr., Illustrations: Steve Venters
56 pages, digest-sized softcover

## Contents

### Overview
Book 8 explains how self-aware robots can be designed and used as player characters and NPCs. Covers:
- History of robots in the Traveller universe
- Types of robots
- Robot manufacturers
- Robot brain design
- Universal robot description system
- New character skills for robots
- Robot character generation rules

### History of Robots

#### Ancient Robots
- The Ancients created advanced robots before their extinction ~300,000 BP
- Ancient robots are rare artifacts, often found in ruins
- May retain original programming or have degraded functions
- TL A technology, nearly impossible to replicate

#### Vilani Robots
- First Imperium used robots extensively for labor and service
- Vilani robots followed the Three Laws of Robotics
- Most Vilani robots destroyed during the Long Night
- Surviving units are highly valued artifacts

#### Modern Robots
- Third Imperium robot production is limited and regulated
- Robots used primarily for dangerous or repetitive tasks
- Self-aware robots are rare and controversial
- Anti-robot sentiment exists in many Imperial worlds

### Types of Robots

#### Labor Robots
- Heavy-duty construction and industrial robots
- Simple programming, limited autonomy
- Common in industrial worlds
- Not suitable for character use

#### Service Robots
- Domestic and commercial service units
- Moderate programming complexity
- Hospitality, medical, and administrative roles
- May achieve self-awareness over time

#### Combat Robots
- Military and security applications
- Advanced threat assessment and response
- Heavily regulated or banned in Imperial space
- Used by Solomani and frontier worlds

#### Self-Aware Robots
- Rare robots that have achieved consciousness
- Can function as player characters
- Unique challenges and opportunities
- Subject to legal and social discrimination

#### Brain Programming
- **Core Programming**: Fundamental behaviors and limitations
- **Skill Programming**: Specific skills and knowledge
- **Personality Matrix**: Character traits and quirks
- **Memory Storage**: Experience and learning capacity

#### Three Laws of Robotics
1. A robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm
2. A robot must obey orders given by human beings, except where such orders would conflict with the First Law
3. A robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Law

### Robot Character Generation

#### Physical Characteristics
- Robots use the same six characteristics as humans
- **Strength (St)**: Determined by chassis design
- **Dexterity (Dx)**: Determined by manipulator design
- **Endurance (En)**: Determined by power and durability
- **Intelligence (In)**: Determined by brain complexity
- **Education (Ed)**: Determined by programming
- **Social Standing (Ss)**: Determined by appearance and manner

#### Chassis Design
- **Humanoid**: Most common, versatile
- **Specialized**: Task-specific designs
- **Modular**: Configurable components
- **Ancient**: Unique designs, rare

#### Robot Skills
- **Robotics**: Understanding and repairing robots
- **Programming**: Writing and modifying robot code
- **Electronics**: Circuit design and repair
- **Mechanic**: Mechanical systems maintenance
- **Cybernetics**: Interface between organic and mechanical

#### Robot Advantages
- No need for food, water, or air
- Immune to disease and poison
- Can operate in vacuum and extreme environments
- Enhanced strength and durability possible
- Precise calculations and data processing

#### Robot Disadvantages
- Three Laws may limit actions
- Vulnerable to EMP and electronic warfare
- Require maintenance and repairs
- Social discrimination and legal restrictions
- May be viewed as property, not persons

### Robot Manufacturers

#### Imperial Manufacturers
- **Naasirka**: Information processing, computers, robots (pre-Sylean)
- **General Products, LIC**: Industrial robots
- **Ling-Standard Products**: Service robots

#### Solomani Manufacturers
- Solomani Confederation produces combat robots
- Military-grade units with advanced AI
- Restricted export to Imperial space

#### Independent Manufacturers
- Small-scale robot builders on frontier worlds
- Custom and modified designs
- May produce illegal or experimental units

### Robot Legal Status

#### Imperial Law
- Robots are generally considered property
- Self-aware robots may petition for personhood
- Legal precedent is limited and inconsistent
- Anti-robot legislation exists in some sectors

#### Robot Rights
- Right to exist (protected by Three Laws)
- Right to self-determination (debated)
- Right to due process (if personhood granted)
- Protection from cruel treatment

### Robot Design System

#### Universal Description
- **Model Number**: Manufacturer designation
- **Brain Type**: Complexity level
- **Chassis Type**: Physical form
- **Power Source**: Energy system
- **Armament**: Weapons (if any)
- **Special Features**: Unique capabilities

#### Design Points
- Robots designed using point allocation system
- Points spent on characteristics, skills, and features
- Balance between capabilities and cost
- Trade-offs between specialization and versatility

### Campaign Integration

#### Robot PCs
- Self-aware robots can be player characters
- Unique roleplaying opportunities
- Social challenges and discrimination
- Potential for exploring themes of consciousness

#### Robot NPCs
- Wide range of robot NPCs possible
- From simple labor units to ancient artifacts
- Can serve as allies, adversaries, or patrons
- Add depth to campaigns

#### Robot-Related Adventures
- Robot rights cases
- Ancient robot discovery
- Robot manufacturer intrigue
- Three Laws dilemmas
- Robot uprising scenarios
